# P0-7 Brief for a lawyer: outbound AI voice calling system (region: India)

Status: DRAFT for the owner to review and send. Not legal advice. This document states what the software does and asks questions. It contains no legal conclusions.
Citations are `file:line` relative to the repo root. [READ] = read in code/docs. [RAN] = checked by a command.

## 1. What the system does (facts)
1. **Purpose and calls.** A backend places outbound phone calls to contacts uploaded into campaigns. The voice conversation (speech-to-text, language model, text-to-speech, telephony, caller number) runs inside the third-party platform **Dograh**; this software only triggers the call by sending the phone number and a context object: `backend/app/services/queue/dialer_worker.py:1105-1110, 1128` [READ]; `docs/CHECKPOINT-10-NOTES.md:40-44` [READ]. Context contains the internal attempt id, contact id, campaign id and campaign name (`dialer_worker.py:1105-1110`).
2. **Region.** Default region `IN`, default timezone `Asia/Kolkata`, dialing allowed only to regions in `ALLOWED_DIAL_REGIONS`, default `["IN"]`: `backend/app/core/config.py:302-307` [READ]. Enforced at contact import (`contact_import_service.py:69`, `campaign_contact_service.py:58`) and at dial eligibility (`eligibility_service.py:126`) [READ].
3. **Calling hours.** Default window 09:00-21:00 (`config.py:315-316`). A hard limit of 09:00-21:00 in the campaign's own timezone that no campaign window may exceed (`config.py:311-312`; rejected on save and clamped at evaluation: `services/calling_window.py:11`) [READ]. The code comment says "NOT legal advice -- a conservative default pending the owner's legal review" (`config.py:309-310`).
4. **Retries.** Defaults: 2 retries, spaced 3600 s then 14400 s (`config.py:317-318`); ceiling 5 retries (`:319`); minimum backoff 300 s (`:320`); maximum 7 days (`:321`) [READ]. Retry evaluation is for never-connected and mid-call-disconnect cases (`models/retry_policy.py:4`). Daily dial cap default 1000 (`config.py:325`).
5. **Suppression (do-not-call) list.** One global table, one row per normalised number, checked before each dial, so a number is blocked in every campaign: `models/suppression.py:1-9`; `eligibility_service.py:69-70` [READ]. Sources recorded: `agent_in_call`, `manual_api` (`models/enums.py:82-86`). Stored in clear as a phone number. **Never purged** by any lifecycle job (`docs/DATA-INVENTORY.md:28`; `data_lifecycle/retention.py:11-13`). Entries are added/imported/removed by admin through the API (`services/suppression_service.py:1-9`); removals are audited.
6. **Registry scrubbing and consent.** "Registry scrubbing (NDNC/DND) and consent management are NOT implemented: no registry credentials exist. Bulk import of a list scrubbed elsewhere is the supported path": `services/suppression_service.py:7-8` [READ]. A search for "disclos", "breach", "caller id" in `backend/app`, `CHECKPOINT-14-NOTES.md`, `CP15-ARCHITECTURE.md` found no AI-disclosure, breach-notification or caller-ID handling code [RAN].
7. **What the caller says.** The script, language, voice and any disclosure wording live in the Dograh workflow, which this backend can neither set nor verify: `docs/CHECKPOINT-10-NOTES.md:40-44, 121` [READ].
8. **Opt-out during a call.** An in-call opt-out can be recorded as a suppression with source `agent_in_call` (`models/enums.py:85`). For Dograh calls no do-not-call/opt-out disposition is mapped: "DEFERRED: do-not-call / opt-out normalization -- Dograh publishes no such code" (`docs/CHECKPOINT-10-NOTES.md:104-105`) [READ].
9. **Recording.** Recording storage is **not implemented**. It is blocked on an open provider-side consent decision: `docs/CHECKPOINT-15A-NOTES.md:20, 113`; `docs/CP15-ARCHITECTURE.md:72, 78-81` [READ]. Whether Dograh records calls is unverified (`CHECKPOINT-10-NOTES.md:121`). A `recording_consent` field exists on call attempts (`models/call_attempt.py:72`) and is filled by the native engine path only (`ai/conversation/orchestrator.py:424,457`).
10. **Stored personal data** [READ, `docs/DATA-INVENTORY.md:28-37`]: contact phone numbers and related fields; suppression numbers; call attempt records; transcript text of calls (one row per line, downloaded from Dograh after the call: `services/telephony/dograh_webhook_service.py:168-183`); AI analysis free text (summary, feedback, key facts, objections, needs) in `call_analysis`; audit log with actor and entity ids; an erasure log holding a keyed hash of an erased number, never the number.
11. **Retention.** Six configurable periods, all required in production, 0 = keep forever; none has a value yet: `config.py:546-555, 580-585`; `docs/phase0/05-decision-B.md`. Contacts are anonymised (phone replaced by a placeholder) counted from campaign close (`retention.py:205-235`). Backups are not edited when data is erased or expires (`DATA-INVENTORY.md:53`).
12. **Erasure and export.** Admin-only API: erase by phone number or contact id, with `suppress` defaulting to true (`api/routes/admin_data.py:35-56`; `schemas/admin_data.py:15`); export of one contact's data (`admin_data.py:58`); legal hold per campaign or contact (`admin_data.py:68-100`) [READ]. Erasure suppresses by default, so the number stays in the suppression table (`CHECKPOINT-15A-NOTES.md:95`).
13. **Third-party processors.** (a) Dograh: receives the phone number and context, holds call audio, provider-side transcripts and run metadata, outside this repo's deletion control; no documented delete API (`DATA-INVENTORY.md:37`; `CHECKPOINT-15A-NOTES.md:102`). (b) The telephony carrier and caller number: configured inside Dograh (`CHECKPOINT-10-NOTES.md:42-44`), identity not known to this repo. (c) The language model and speech services inside Dograh; their vendors and data locations are not visible to this repo [READ]. (d) Analysis/QA: provider `mock` or `dograh_qa` (`config.py:29, 239`) [READ]. Data-centre locations for all of these: not established in this repo.
14. **Redis.** Holds queue jobs (ids), counters, kill switch/heartbeats and a short-lived conversation-memory cache (TTL 3600 s) that can contain captured entities and the last agent utterance; erasure/retention do not delete those keys (`DATA-INVENTORY.md:35`; `CHECKPOINT-15A-NOTES.md:103`) [READ].
15. **Access.** Two fixed accounts, `admin` and `operator`, no user table; all authenticated users see all data (`config.py:72-75`; `CHECKPOINT-11-NOTES.md:59`) [READ].

## 2. Open questions for the lawyer
Each question says why it matters and which setting or code it controls. Nothing here states what the law is.
1. **Do-not-call registry scrubbing.** Which registry or registries apply to these calls, and how often must a number list be checked against them? Why: today there is no scrubbing code (fact 6). Controls: `suppression_service.py` bulk import; any future scrub job.
2. **Registration and caller identity.** Are there registration, number-series or caller-ID display requirements for automated or AI-voice outbound calls, and who must hold them? Why: the number is chosen inside Dograh (fact 7, 13b). Controls: Dograh workflow settings; a pre-launch checklist.
3. **AI disclosure and consent at call start.** Is the call required to say it is automated or AI, and what wording and language? Is any consent needed before the conversation continues? Why: wording sits in the Dograh script, not in this code (fact 7). Controls: the Dograh workflow; `CALLING_ENGINE` is Dograh-only in production.
4. **Opt-out handling.** What must happen when a person says stop during a call, and within what time must the number be blocked? Why: there is no automatic opt-out code mapping for Dograh (fact 8). Controls: `SuppressionSource.AGENT_IN_CALL`, dial-time check (`eligibility_service.py:69`).
5. **Recording consent and storage.** If calls are recorded (by Dograh or by us), what consent and notice are needed, who may listen, and for how long may recordings be stored? Why: recording is blocked on this answer (fact 9). Controls: decision 9 in `CP15-ARCHITECTURE.md:78`; `ENABLE_CALL_RECORDING_UPLOAD` on Dograh.
6. **Permitted calling hours.** Are the hours 09:00-21:00 and their timezone rule acceptable, and do they differ by contact type, state or day? Why: they are code defaults flagged as pending legal review (fact 3). Controls: `HARD_CALLING_WINDOW_START/END`, `DEFAULT_CALLING_WINDOW_*` (`config.py:311-316`).
7. **Retry limits.** How many attempts per contact, how far apart, and over what total period are acceptable? Why: default 2 retries with 1 h and 4 h spacing, ceiling 5 (fact 4). Controls: `DEFAULT_MAX_RETRIES`, `DEFAULT_RETRY_SPACING_SECONDS`, `MAX_RETRIES_CEILING`.
8. **Retention minimums and maximums.** For each of transcripts, analysis text, contact data, audit log, processed-event ids, run history: is there a period that must be kept, a period that must not be exceeded, and a start event? Why: production will not start until all six are set (fact 11). Controls: the six `RETENTION_*_DAYS` settings; see `05-decision-B.md`.
9. **Suppression list retention.** May the do-not-call list keep numbers indefinitely, and on what basis, given erasure requests? Why: the table is never purged and erasure suppresses by default (facts 5, 12). Controls: `ErasureRequest.suppress`; the "never purged" rule.
10. **Cross-border processing.** Where are Dograh, the telephony carrier, the language and speech vendors and any backups located, and are transfers out of India permitted on what terms? Why: locations are unknown to this repo (fact 13). Controls: choice of Dograh deployment/vendors; the backup destination.
11. **Processor agreements.** What contractual terms are needed with Dograh and its sub-processors, including deletion on request? Why: no delete API exists on Dograh's side (fact 13). Controls: vendor choice; whether erasure can be completed.
12. **Breach notification.** Who must be told, how fast and in what form if personal data is exposed? Why: no breach-handling code or runbook was found (fact 6). Controls: a runbook (not yet written); audit log and access model (fact 15).
13. **Erasure request handling.** Who may request erasure, what must be erased, in what time, and what may be kept (for example the suppression row, audit rows, older backups)? Why: backups still contain erased people until they expire; Dograh copies are outside our reach (facts 12, 13). Controls: `POST /api/v1/admin/data/erasure`, legal hold, `reapply_erasures` after restore.
14. **Access by staff.** Is shared admin/operator access acceptable, or must access be per person and logged by name? Why: two fixed accounts today (fact 15). Controls: auth model (see `06-decision-C.md`).
15. **Legal hold.** In what situations must data be kept despite retention or erasure requests? Why: the hold feature exists but its use rules are unset (fact 12). Controls: `admin_data.py:68-100`.

- Legal name, type and registration of the business entity that will place the calls and its data-controller/processor role.
- The phone number series or numbers that will be used and who holds them (carrier name).
- The use case: who is called (existing customers, leads, purchased lists), the source of the numbers and what permission was given when they were collected.
- The exact call script, language(s), AI voice description and the in-call opt-out wording.
- The Dograh account details: plan, data location, whether recording is on, the sub-processors Dograh uses.
- Expected volume (the stated baseline is 100,000 contacts per month: `docs/CHECKPOINT-09-NOTES.md:499`) and the planned start date.
- Backup destination and who has access (`docs/CHECKPOINT-15B-NOTES.md`).
- The owner's answers to the retention questions in `05-decision-B.md`.
